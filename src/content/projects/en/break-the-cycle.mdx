## What it is

**Break the Cycle** is a web project developed with **React and Vite** focusing on raising awareness and providing guidance on **domestic and family violence against women**.

The project's goal is to transform critical information regarding violence, rights, and ways to seek help into a simple, organized, and accessible web experience.

Rather than functioning solely as an institutional landing page, the project was structured into distinct content areas, allowing it to approach the topic progressively: identifying violence, understanding rights, reporting channels, support, and guidance following a report.

## Objective

The main objective of the project is to use technology as a tool for **information and awareness**.

The application presents content related to domestic violence, including:

- definition and contextualization of domestic violence;
- main types of violence;
- information about the Maria da Penha Law;
- support and reporting channels;
- guidance related to the period after making a report;
- information on rights and ways to seek help.

The homepage reinforces the message that violence is never the victim's fault and showcases pathways for individuals to seek support whenever they feel ready.

## Content structure

The project features pages organized by specific themes, including:

- home;
- support and rights;
- reporting channels;
- guidance after reporting.

This division ensures that each topic has its own dedicated space, preventing all information from being crammed onto a single page.

## Homepage

The homepage was built as a sequence of informative sections.

The implemented elements include:

- main project presentation;
- introduction to domestic violence;
- visual representation of the cycle of violence;
- statistics section;
- overview of the main types of violence;
- support channels;
- highlight on the Maria da Penha Law;
- final welcoming and guiding message.

The structure utilizes React components and keeps data organization separated from the presentation layer.

## Types of violence

One of the central parts of the application is the presentation of different types of violence.

The project addresses:

- physical violence;
- psychological violence;
- sexual violence;
- property/patrimonial violence;
- moral violence.

Each category provides examples to help visitors recognize different forms of violence that can occur within relationships and family environments.

The presentation utilizes a **responsive slider**, adapting the number of cards displayed according to the screen size.

## Support channels

The project also provides a specific area to facilitate access to support services.

The channels featured in the application include:

- **190**, for emergency situations;
- **180**, related to the Women's Central Hotline;
- information on psychological support;
- information related to women's rights.

Emergency and helpline numbers are presented as click-to-call telephone links, easing access especially on mobile devices.

## Maria da Penha Law

The application features a dedicated section for **Law No. 11,340/2006 — Maria da Penha Law**.

The purpose of this section is to contextualize the legislation and highlight that legal mechanisms exist for preventing and combating domestic and family violence against women.

The application directs visitors to additional information through the project's own navigation structure.

## Components and organization

The project uses **React** to structure the interface into reusable components.

The code organization separates pages, components, styles, and data.

The homepage, for instance, maintains the content used across sections in a separate data file, while the component itself remains solely responsible for rendering that information.

This approach simplifies maintenance and allows content updates without cluttering the component's visual structure.

## Interface

The interface utilizes:

- **Bootstrap**;
- **React Bootstrap**;
- Custom CSS;
- responsive components;
- cards for content organization;
- slider powered by **Swiper**.

The project also uses custom breakpoints to adapt the number of elements shown in the slider based on the device.

On smaller screens, for example, the content transitions to displaying fewer cards at a time, improving the overall browsing experience.

## Learnings

One of the key takeaways from this project was realizing that an informative application must consider not only the technical implementation but also the way content is delivered.

In this scenario, information architecture plays an essential role in the user experience itself.

Separating pages, components, data, and styles also helped in experimenting with a cleaner, more organized structure for React applications.
